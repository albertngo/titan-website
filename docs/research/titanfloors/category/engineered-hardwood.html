Engineered Hardwood Collections

Engineered hardwood is a versatile and durable flooring option that combines the natural beauty of real wood with enhanced stability. Made with a solid wood veneer layer on top of high-quality plywood or fiberboard, it resists moisture and temperature changes better than solid hardwood. Available in a variety of wood species, finishes, and styles, engineered hardwood brings timeless elegance to any room while offering a long-lasting, low-maintenance solution for your floors.

Questions About Engineered Hardwood?

We’ve got all the answers—no woodpecker required!

What is engineered hardwood?
Engineered hardwood is like hardwood's modern cousin—real wood on top, but with a plywood base underneath. It’s stylish, stable, and ready for action.
How is engineered hardwood different from solid hardwood?
Solid hardwood is one solid piece of wood, while engineered hardwood has a real wood veneer over a sturdy plywood core. It’s more stable with temperature and humidity changes but can’t be refinished as often.
Is engineered hardwood real wood?
Yes, the top layer of engineered hardwood is made from real wood, giving it the same appearance and texture as solid hardwood. The layers beneath provide structural support and stability.
How long do engineered hardwood floors last?
With proper care and maintenance, engineered hardwood floors can last anywhere from 20 to 30 years, depending on the quality of the materials and the thickness of the veneer layer.
Is engineered hardwood durable?
Yes, engineered hardwood is quite durable, especially in areas with fluctuating temperatures and humidity. Its layered construction makes it more resistant to warping and wear than solid hardwood, making it a great choice for high-traffic areas.
How do I maintain and clean engineered hardwood floors?
Easy care is the name of the game. Sweep, vacuum, and mop with a damp cloth or a cleaner designed for hardwood. Avoid soaking or harsh chemicals, and keep spills wiped up.
Is engineered hardwood resistant to moisture and humidity?
Engineered hardwood handles humidity better than solid wood. It’s less prone to expanding or contracting with moisture changes, making it great for variable climates.
